What the four measures mean

Risk of incursion is rated on six levels and impact on five. The cards and table only show the levels that apply to these diseases, so the whole scale is here, from lowest to highest. Control measures and response options show how many checklist items still need review.

  • Risk of incursion Chance of arriving in the UK. Lowest first Negligible Very low Low Medium High Very high
  • Impact Harm caused if it arrives. Lowest first Insignificant Minor Significant Major Severe
  • Control measures Preventing a disease from arriving. The bar shows how many of the checklist items still need review. More red is worse
  • Response options Responding if it arrives. The bar shows how many of the checklist items still need review. More red is worse
How the priority score is worked out

The priority score uses all four measures. This is an illustration for the prototype, not the final model. A higher score means we should act sooner.

  1. Threat. Risk of incursion (1 to 6) multiplied by impact (1 to 5), up to 30.
  2. Preparedness gap. The share of control measure items and response option items that still need review, averaged, from 0 (nothing needs review) to 1 (everything does).
  3. Priority score. Threat multiplied by 1 plus the preparedness gap, up to 60.

A disease with a very high risk and a severe impact, and where everything needs review, scores 60. The same threat with nothing needing review scores 30.
