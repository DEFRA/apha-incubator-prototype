What the three measures mean

Risk of incursion is rated on six levels, and impact and preparedness on five. The cards and table only show the levels that apply to these diseases, so the whole scale is here. Preparedness brings together control measures and response options.

  • Risk of incursion Chance of arriving in the UK. Lowest first Negligible Very low Low Medium High Very high
  • Impact Harm caused if it arrives. Lowest first Insignificant Minor Significant Major Severe
  • Preparedness How ready we are to prevent a disease from arriving and to respond if it does. Most prepared first Totally prepared Largely prepared Moderately prepared Slightly prepared Not at all prepared
How the priority score is worked out

The priority score uses all four measures: risk of incursion, impact, control measures and response options. Control measures and response options are shown together as preparedness. This is an illustration for the prototype, not the final model. A higher score means a higher priority.

  1. Threat. Risk of incursion (1 to 6) multiplied by impact (1 to 5), up to 30.
  2. Preparedness gap. The share of control measure items and response option items that still need review, averaged, from 0 (nothing needs review) to 1 (everything does).
  3. Priority score. Threat multiplied by 1 plus the preparedness gap, up to 60.

The preparedness status comes from the gap. Under 0.2 is totally prepared, 0.2 to 0.44 largely, 0.45 to 0.64 moderately, 0.65 to 0.84 slightly, and 0.85 or more not at all prepared.
