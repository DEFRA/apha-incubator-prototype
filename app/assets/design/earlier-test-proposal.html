What to test first

The team is leaning towards offering both a by-disease and a by-layer view. This page proposes two separate tests: a concept test on how users get from the priority view into each, and a risk slice test on the pages they land on.

"Layer" means risk, impact or preparedness (the team also says "slice"). All data is illustrative and the diseases are fictional.

The two tests at a glance

They answer different questions, so neither depends on the other. Whichever way the routing goes, users land on a disease risk page, and the risk across diseases page is the layer view for risk.

Test 1: Concept test: how users route between the two views

Everyone starts on the priority view, because priority is the top level. The question is how the by-disease and by-layer views sit under it. Three ways to do it, shown here with the priority view as step 1. The yellow highlight is the part that does the routing.

Concept 1: A switch

A "View by" switch above the list: Disease or Layer. Disease is the default and shows the ranked list. Layer shows risk, impact and preparedness, each listing all diseases.

  1. Step 1Priority view

    Switch above the ranked list

    • Step 2 – choose oneDisease page

      Click a disease

    • orLayer page

      Switch to "Layer"

Why test it: an explicit control is easy to explain. It gives both views equal weight. The question is whether users notice it and understand "layer".

Concept 2: The table is the router

The priority view is a table. Priority (band and score) is the first column and the default sort, with a column for each layer that feeds it. Click a disease name to open the disease page. Click a layer column heading (Risk, Impact, Control, Response) to see that layer across all diseases. No separate control.

  1. Step 1Priority view

    Priority first. Layer headings link to the layer pages

    • Step 2 – choose oneDisease page

      Click a disease name

    • orLayer page

      Click a column heading

Why test it: it matches the grid of diseases and layers the data already forms, so it needs no extra control. The risk is that heading links are not obvious.

Concept 3: Layer tabs lead (challenger, see recommendation)

Tabs run across the top: Priority, Risk, Impact, Preparedness. Priority is the first tab and the default. Each layer tab lists all diseases. A disease opens its page from any tab.

  1. Step 1Priority view

    Layer tabs are the main navigation

    • Step 2 – choose oneDisease page

      Click a disease

    • orLayer page

      Click a layer tab

Why test it: it treats the layer view as an equal. That suits people who own a layer. The risk is that priority becomes just one tab, and layers without data show as empty tabs.

Tasks (first click)

Show each concept as a static screen. Ask where they would click first, then what they expect to see.

1"Which disease should we act on first?"
2"See how all the diseases compare on risk."
3"See everything we know about Sprouting hoof disease."
4"Find which diseases would have the highest impact."

What we watch for

  • Does the first click go to the right place?
  • Can they say what the switch or the headings do before clicking?
  • Do they ask for the other route without being prompted?
  • Which describes how they work: by disease, or by layer? Record each participant's role and what they usually look at, to see whether role predicts preference.
  • At the end: which do they prefer, and why?

Recommendation for Test 1

Compare Concept 1 (switch) and Concept 2 (table as router). Both lead with priority, which is the agreed top level, and differ only in how the second view is reached. Keep Concept 3 as a short discussion prompt at the end ("what if layers were the main navigation?"), not a full variant, to keep the test small. Rotate the order of concepts 1 and 2 across participants.

Test 2: Risk slice test: a disease risk page and a risk across diseases page

Yes. The risk slice is two pages. Risk across diseases is the layer page: every disease, its risk level and what changed. Disease risk is one disease through the risk layer: why it is at this level, how it has changed and where the data comes from. These are the same destination pages in every routing option, so this test can run whatever the concept test decides.

Questions it answers

  • Do text tags ("Risk increased") work better than arrows?
  • Is a "Latest changes" view worth having, or is sorting enough?
  • Do cards or a list suit comparing diseases?
  • Does the chart add anything beyond the table?
  • Do users find the reason for a change, and the source?
  • Can users move between the two pages without getting lost?

Hypotheses 1 to 5 and 7 in the design brief.

Impact, if the data is ready

The same two-page pattern works for impact: an impact across diseases page and a disease impact page (High or Very high, plus what is affected). If impact data is ready for design, add it as a stretch in this test. If not, keep the impact line on the pages as in the current wireframes, and test the pattern once it is.

Decisions needed from the team

  • Which concepts go forward? Proposed: concepts 1 and 2 as the comparison, concept 3 as a discussion prompt.
  • One session or two? Proposed: one session with the concept test first. If they must be separate, the risk test should not run first.
  • Is impact in the risk test? Only if its data is ready for design.
  • Who takes part? We want people who work by disease and people who work by layer. How many, and how do we recruit for both?
  • What do we call the second view? "Layer" and "slice" are team words. Users may call it something else.
  • How do we explain the illustrative data? A short line at the start, so participants don't read the numbers as real.

This is a proposal for the team to challenge, not a decision.
