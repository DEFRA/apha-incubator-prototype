Option C · For the design team, not for participants

Bars under the risk and impact levels

Risk of incursion and impact each show a level word in a colour block, and a grey bar under it. Now there is also a key for all five levels at the bottom of the page. This page compares the table with the bars and without them, to see whether the bars are still needed.

Decided We removed the bars on 11 November 2026, to test it. Option C now has no bars under the risk and impact levels, and the key at the bottom does that job. If research shows people do not know where a level sits on the scale, we add something back. The checklist columns (control measures and response options) keep their red segment bars, because they show something different: how many items need review.

What the bar adds, and what it repeats

  • It repeats the word and the colour. "Major" and the bar at 80% say the same thing. Both come from the same 1 to 5 rating.
  • It does add the position on the scale. It is the only thing in the cell that shows that "Minor" is second from the bottom, and not the lowest. The key at the bottom of the page now says that too, but it is a long way from the cell.
  • It is grey, so it has no colour of its own. The word's colour block carries the colour. The bar is hidden from screen readers, so it adds nothing for them.
  • It makes the two columns look different from the checklist columns. Grey fill from the left, against red segments for items to review. They mean different things, but they look alike at a glance.

Which to choose

The case for removing them

  • The word and the colour already carry the level, and the bar says it a third time
  • The table is busy, and this is the easiest thing to take out without losing information
  • It gives each kind of column its own look: words for ratings, bars for counts
  • It follows the earlier steer of "less is more"

The case for keeping them

  • The bar is the only thing in the cell that shows where the level sits on the scale
  • The key does the same job, but it is at the bottom, so people must scroll to it and then match words across
  • The concern that started the key was that people do not know "Minor" is not the lowest level

Where I lean: remove them, and test it. The key now covers the scale, and the cell is calmer without a third cue. The thing to check in research is whether people read "Minor" or "Likely" as the lowest or highest level. If they do, the options are to move the key closer to the table, or to bring the bars back.

Still to decide

  • Which version. Decided: without the bars, to test. The with-bars version stays above as the alternative.
  • Where the key sits. It is under the band key at the moment. If the bars go, a key nearer the table, or a link from the column headings, may be needed.
  • Test it. Ask someone where "Minor" sits, without showing them the key.
