Option C · For the design team, not for participants

Arranging the priority cell

Three ways to show a change in the priority score without splitting the text. Only the Priority cell and the card change. Nothing is added to the other columns.

The problem

At the moment the cell reads like this, top to bottom:

Disease PriorityScore out of 5
Himalayan Dragon Pox Prioritise Score 4.1 Score decreasedfrom 4.4

The summary is "Score 4.1 … from 4.4". The outlined tag sits in the middle of it, and changes the style mid-sentence. That makes the two values hard to read as one thought.

All three options below keep the two values together, and put the signal of the change before or beside them, never between them.

All three also drop the outlined tag. That makes the table calmer, because it is one less coloured box in the column, and the priority band stays the loudest thing. The words "up" and "down" or "increased" and "decreased" are always written, so colour and shape are never the only cue.

Option 1: labels together, then the summary

The band and the change label sit together at the top, as two labels. The score and the old score follow as one plain line underneath.

Disease PriorityScore out of 5
Sprouting hoof disease Act now Score 4.8
Himalayan Dragon Pox Prioritise Decreased Score 4.1, was 4.4
Rainbow tail disease Prioritise Score 3.9
Wing wobble Maintain Increased Score 1.4, was 1.2

On a card

  • Himalayan Dragon Pox

    Decreased Score 4.1 out of 5, was 4.4

    A moderate chance of arriving and high impact. Neither our control measures nor our response options are strong.

Good for

  • Keeping the summary to one plain line
  • The change label is easy to find, and it is the same shape as before
  • A screen reader says "Decreased. Score 4.1, was 4.4"

Watch for

  • Two boxes stacked in the cell, so the cell is still busy
  • The change label is separated from the old score it refers to
  • Only changed rows have a second label, so rows have different heights

Option 2: an arrow in its own column

A small arrow sits to the left of the text, in a gutter of its own. The text is two plain lines in one style: "Score 4.1" and "Down from 4.4". Rows without a change keep the empty gutter, so the text lines up down the column.

Disease PriorityScore out of 5
Sprouting hoof disease Act now Score 4.8
Himalayan Dragon Pox Prioritise Score 4.1Down from 4.4
Rainbow tail disease Prioritise Score 3.9
Wing wobble Maintain Score 1.4Up from 1.2

On a card

  • Himalayan Dragon Pox

    Score 4.1 out of 5Down from 4.4

    A moderate chance of arriving and high impact. Neither our control measures nor our response options are strong.

Good for

  • The text is one block, so it reads as a single summary
  • Quick to scan: the arrows show which rows changed, and which way, without reading
  • No coloured box, so the column is calmer
  • A screen reader says "Score 4.1. Down from 4.4", and ignores the arrow

Watch for

  • It reverses the earlier decision of "text only, no arrows". The earlier worry was that an arrow's direction could be mistaken for "better" or "worse". Here the word "Up" or "Down" sits beside it, and for priority, up means more urgent. Test it
  • The arrow colour (red up, green down) suggests a judgement. It could be neutral grey
  • A small shape to build, and to make sure it prints

Option 3: text only, one word in bold

No tag and no arrow. The change is a second plain line, "Decreased from 4.4", and the first word is bold. The bold word comes first, so nothing changes style in the middle of the sentence.

Disease PriorityScore out of 5
Sprouting hoof disease Act now Score 4.8
Himalayan Dragon Pox Prioritise Score 4.1Decreased from 4.4
Rainbow tail disease Prioritise Score 3.9
Wing wobble Maintain Score 1.4Increased from 1.2

On a card

  • Himalayan Dragon Pox

    Score 4.1 out of 5Decreased from 4.4

    A moderate chance of arriving and high impact. Neither our control measures nor our response options are strong.

Good for

  • The calmest of the three, with no extra box or shape
  • The cell is one block of text, so it reads like a sentence
  • Nothing new to build, and nothing to explain. A screen reader says "Score 4.1. Decreased from 4.4"

Watch for

  • The weakest signal. A change is easy to miss when scanning a long list
  • The bold word is the only thing that sets a changed row apart
  • It doesn't show direction at a glance. You have to read the word

Suggestion

Option 2. It keeps the summary as one plain block, which is what was wrong with the tag, and the arrow tells you which rows changed and which way without any reading. It is also quieter than the tag it replaces. Pair every arrow with the words "Up" or "Down", and test that people read "up" as "more urgent".

If the team would rather not bring arrows back, option 3 is the calmest fallback. The cost is that a change is easier to miss.

To decide

  • Arrow colour. Red up and green down, or neutral grey?
  • Wording. "Up from 4.4" and "Down from 4.4", or "Increased" and "Decreased"?
  • Unchanged rows. Show nothing, as here, or a quiet "No change"?
