Priority view: three design options

Three structures for the same question: "what should we act on first?" A is a table, B is cards grouped by action, and C lets people switch between the two.

Option C is the version we developed further in the 11 November 2026 design session. Options A and B still reflect the earlier design. They have not changed, so they no longer match C. They keep the earlier scores out of 5, ranks and wording, and will be brought into line with C later. Until then, differences between A, B and C are about more than structure.

Option A: ranked table

Open option A Earlier design

One row per disease, in priority order. Priority comes first, then the four measures: risk and impact (threat), then control measures and response options (preparedness). Rules separate the three groups. Column headings sort.

Best for

  • Comparing diseases across a measure ("who is least prepared?")
  • People who work by layer, who read down a column
  • Growing to more diseases, with sorting and filtering

Watch for

  • Needs a wider page. Dense on small screens
  • Users must work out why a disease is in its band

Option B: grouped by action

Open option B Earlier design

Diseases are grouped under Act now, Prioritise, Monitor and Maintain. Each disease is a card with a one-line reason, then the four measures. No table, so the standard page width works.

Best for

  • Answering "what do I do?" without reading numbers
  • People briefing others, who want the reason in words
  • Small screens, where it reads as a list

Watch for

  • Hard to compare one measure across diseases
  • Needs someone to write the one-line reasons
  • Hides the ranking if bands are empty or uneven

Option C: table or cards, your choice

Open option C Developed further

One page with a "View as" switch: Cards (the grouped idea from option B) or Table (the idea from option A). The choice is kept in the address (?view=cards), and sorting is kept when you switch. On small screens it starts on cards. To print or save both views as a PDF, open option-c.html?view=both. See how people move around between priority, each measure and each disease.

What is different from A and B:

  • The priority score is risk × impact, out of 25. Control measures and response options do not change it.
  • The table has six columns: Disease, Priority, Risk of incursion, Impact, Control measures and Response options. Nothing is ranked or numbered. The list is sorted by priority score, highest first.
  • Risk and impact show matrix names (for example "Almost certain" and "Major") and not numbers.
  • Control measures and response options are checklists. Each shows "N of M need review" and a bar with red segments for the items to review. There is no 1 to 5 score.
  • The four priority actions are Act now (15 or more), Prioritise (10 to 14), Monitor (4 to 9) and Maintain (1 to 3). Tags are in sentence case.
  • Every score has a "Changed" date. When a score moved, the table says "Up from" or "Down from" and the cards say "Previous score" with a "Score increased" or "Score decreased" tag.
  • Disease names are plain text. A "More details" link sits under the name in the table and after the summary on each card. For the two Act now diseases it reads "Act now: see why this is a priority".
  • A key at the bottom explains the priority actions and the four measures. It replaces the hints on the cards.
  • There is a sixth fictional disease, Scaly claw fever, with a "Confirmed in the UK" state.
  • The "Look in more detail" section, the summary and the explainer paragraph are gone. The measure pages are reached from the service navigation.

Best for

  • Letting people choose how they work: compare across diseases, or read by action
  • Learning which view people go for, and when, from one prototype

Watch for

  • An extra control to notice and understand
  • Sorting only applies to the table. Cards are grouped by priority action
  • Two views to build, check and keep in step
  • It now differs from A and B in scoring and wording, so a comparison with them is not like for like yet
  • Control measures and response options no longer change the score. We do not yet know how preparedness should link into priority
  • Several figures are assumed: the number of checklist items, how often scores change and the cut-offs for each priority action

What C tells us

A and B each test one structure. C tests whether people need a choice at all. It also lets us test the new wording and layout.

  • Which view do they land on, and do they switch?
  • Do they switch for a reason, such as comparing one measure or briefing a colleague?
  • Do they notice the switch without being told?
  • Does having both make the page feel more helpful, or more complicated?
  • Is "N of M need review" read as good or bad news?
  • Does "Changed" with a date mean the score changed, or that it was last checked?
  • Do people expect the "Act now" link to give actions, and do they try to select the plain-text name?

If one view is rarely used, drop the other and keep the page simpler.

Decisions made on option C

Each page sets out the options we looked at and the decision.

  • Arranging the priority cell: how to show a change in score without splitting the text. We chose a small arrow at the end of the "Up from" or "Down from" line
  • Showing checklist progress in a cell: "N of M need review" with red segments, for control measures and response options
  • Bars under the risk and impact levels: whether risk and impact keep their grey bars. We removed them, to test
  • Showing the hint text on the cards: why the hints were replaced by a key at the bottom and "More details" links
  • Showing when a priority score changed: a "Changed" date under every score, chosen over a "Last changed" column or a comparison over a period

Rules that make it work

  • The service navigation is on every page and marks the current page in text as well as style (aria-current="page").
  • Every link to a disease goes to the same disease page, wherever it appears. On Priority the disease name is plain text and "More details" is the link.
  • Each measure page uses the same disease names, priority actions and colours as Priority, so people recognise where they are.
  • Table and Cards stay on Priority. The "View as" switch never takes you to another page.
  • Column headings only sort. Routes to other pages are names and navigation, so one control never does two jobs.

Questions this flow raises

  • Do people go to a disease or a measure first, and does it depend on their role?
  • Do they understand that Risk, Impact, Control measures and Response options each show every disease?
  • Do they cross between a disease and a measure, or go back to Priority each time?
  • Should the disease page repeat all four measures, or only link to them?
  • Is there content behind the "Act now" link, and does it match "see why this is a priority"?
  • The wireframes ask two questions this flow depends on: is each layer a section on one page or a separate page (2C), and do the risk views stay as a "View" switch or get replaced (1D)? This flow assumes separate pages.

Width and spacing

The standard GOV.UK page is 960px wide. Six columns with hints and bars need about 1,000px of table, so the original felt cramped and wrapped headings and tags.

Recommendation: keep one page, use a wider container for the table. Option A uses 1,200px with more cell padding and rules between the priority, threat and preparedness columns. Text stays at two-thirds width so lines remain readable.

We would not move the table to its own full-width page yet:

  • Priority is the top level. Putting it a click away hides the main answer from the first look
  • It is a spacing problem, not a quantity problem. Five rows and six columns fit one wide page
  • A second page splits the bands and sources from the table they explain

Revisit if priority 1 grows past about 8 to 10 diseases, or more measures are added. Then show the top few on this page and link to a full-width page with filtering and sorting.

Below about 1,050px wide, option A scrolls sideways inside a labelled region rather than clipping.

Decisions so far

These were made earlier and describe A and B. Where option C has moved on, it says so. The latest decisions are listed under Decisions made on option C.

  • There is no summary at the top of the page. It had to cover every section, so it was too long. Each card keeps a one-line reason. There is also no paragraph explaining the scores above the list, because it repeated the band key. Option C has a key at the bottom instead.
  • Source is not repeated at the top. It is in "Where this information comes from" at the bottom.
  • One header row on the table. The Threat and Preparedness group headings were removed. A sentence above the table explains the grouping, and rules separate priority, threat and preparedness. Test whether people miss it.
  • The sorted column has a light tint. The arrow and the screen reader state say the same thing, so colour is not the only cue.
  • Links in the table don't change colour once visited. Hover and focus still change.
  • The matrix option was dropped. People don't think disease-first across the top, and it pushed the content down.
  • Two routes out. In options A and B, disease names link to a disease page. Risk, Impact, Control measures and Response options are in the top navigation and in a "Look in more detail" line. Column headings only sort. Option C has dropped "Look in more detail" and made the disease name plain text with a "More details" link.
  • Change is shown for priority only. In options A and B, an outlined tag ("Score increased" or "Score decreased") with "from 4.4" under the score, text only, no arrows. Unchanged diseases show nothing. Option C has moved on: every score has a "Changed" date, the table has a small arrow after "Up from" or "Down from", and the cards keep the tag. Changes in risk, impact, control measures and response options belong on their own pages.
  • Impact shows the four areas: Public health, Animal welfare, Wider economy and society, International trade. A disease can have more than one, shown as a short list.

Testing

We are taking option C forward to test. Its table and cards hold the structures of both A and B, so one page covers both. The way to run a session is for the user researcher and the wider team to decide. See notes for the user researcher.

Options A and B have not had the design changes made to C, so they are not planned for testing as they are. If the team wants a like-for-like comparison later, bring them into line with C first.

Starting on cards, starting on the table or alternating between them is for the researcher and team to decide. Either view can be opened directly: option-c.html?view=cards or option-c.html?view=table.
