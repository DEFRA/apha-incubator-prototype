What the priority actions mean

  • Act now Score of 15 or more. The main focus for resources.
  • Prioritise Score of 10 to 14. Close the preparedness gaps.
  • Monitor Score of 4 to 9. Review readiness and watch the risk.
  • Maintain Score of 1 to 3. Keep current arrangements.

What the four measures mean

Risk of incursion and impact are each rated on five levels. The cards and table only show the levels that apply to these diseases, so the whole scale is here, from lowest to highest. Control measures and response options show how many checklist items still need review.

  • Risk of incursion Chance of arriving in the UK. Lowest first Rare Unlikely Moderate Likely Almost certain
  • Impact Harm caused if it arrives. Lowest first Insignificant Minor Significant Major Severe
  • Control measures Preventing a disease from arriving. The bar shows how many of the checklist items still need review. More red is worse
  • Response options Responding if it arrives. The bar shows how many of the checklist items still need review. More red is worse
How the priority score is worked out

Priority is risk of incursion multiplied by impact, giving a score from 1 to 25. A higher score means we should act sooner. Each is rated from 1 to 5.

  • Risk of incursion: how likely the disease is to reach the UK. Rare, unlikely, moderate, likely or almost certain
  • Impact: how much harm it would do if it arrived. Insignificant, minor, significant, major or severe

Control measures and response options show how many items still need review. They sit beside the priority score and do not change it.
