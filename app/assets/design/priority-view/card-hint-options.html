Option C · For the design team, not for participants

Showing the hint text on the cards

Each card label has a hint under it, such as "Chance of arriving in the UK". It is repeated on every card, and it makes the cards look busy. This page compares ways to keep the hint available without showing it all the time.

Decided We chose option 4, one panel above the cards on 11 November 2026, to cut the repetition without adding 24 buttons. It is in Option C, closed by default. The other options are kept here as the alternatives we considered, and the options 2 and 3 buttons still work if you want to try them.

What "accessible" needs here

A tooltip that appears on hover only is not accessible. Keyboard, touch and screen reader users cannot get to it. The GOV.UK Design System has no tooltip component. An accessible version has to meet these:

  • Works from the keyboard. It is a button, reached with Tab, and opened with Enter or Space.
  • Works on touch. It opens on tap, not hover, and the target is at least 24px (WCAG 2.2 target size). These are 28px.
  • Can be dismissed. Escape closes it, and so does moving focus away or tapping elsewhere (WCAG 1.4.13).
  • Stays put. It does not vanish while someone is reading it, and it does not cover the thing it explains.
  • Is announced. A screen reader reads the hint out when it opens, using a status region.
  • Has a name. The button says what it is for, such as "More information about Risk of incursion", not just "?".
  • Colour is not the only cue. The button has a visible border and a "?" or "i" shape, and a focus style that is not only colour.
  • The hint is not the only place. Anything important must also be on the page, because some people will never open it.

Option 1: always show the hint

What Option C has now. The hint is plain text under every label, so nothing is hidden.

Good for

  • Nothing to find or open, and it works for everyone
  • No JavaScript

Watch for

  • The same four hints repeat on every card. Six cards means 24 lines of repeated text
  • It is the busy version you want to move away from

Option 2: a "?" button that opens the hint (a toggletip)

A small button beside the label. Selecting it opens the hint in a bubble underneath. Escape, a second press, or moving away closes it. Try it.

Good for

  • The card is cleaner, and the hint is one press away, next to the label it explains
  • The pattern is well known, and fully keyboard and screen reader friendly
  • The bubble sits over blank space, so it hides nothing important

Watch for

  • 24 buttons on the page (4 on each of 6 cards). That is more to tab through, and more small icons to look at
  • It needs JavaScript, so it should fall back to showing the hint if the script does not run
  • People may never open it, so the label has to make sense alone
  • The bubble covers whatever sits under the label, as in the screenshot of the Control measures button: it hides the count and part of the bar while it is open

Option 3: a "?" that opens the hint in place (a disclosure)

The same button, but the hint opens inline under the label and pushes the content down. It uses the browser's own details element, so it works without JavaScript.

Good for

  • The simplest accessible build. No script, and the browser handles keyboard and screen readers
  • Nothing can cover other content

Watch for

  • The same 24 buttons as option 2
  • Opening one moves everything below it, and unevens the two cards in a row
  • The hint stays open until you close it, so a card can end up with four open

Option 4: explain the four measures once, above the cards

The cards carry just the label. One "What do these measures mean?" panel above the list holds all four hints. It mirrors the table, where the hints appear once in the column headings, not on every row.

Good for

  • The cleanest cards, with no repeated text and no extra buttons
  • One control instead of 24, and it uses the standard GOV.UK details component
  • No JavaScript, and it matches how the table works
  • The level key at the bottom of the page already works this way

Watch for

  • It is not next to the label, so people need to remember it or scroll back
  • Closed by default, so many people will not open it. The labels have to make sense without it
  • It could sit open by default, or the hints could go in the intro text instead

One more, to avoid

A hover-only tooltip, including the browser's title attribute. It does not appear on keyboard focus or touch, many screen readers ignore it, and it can disappear when the pointer moves. It fails WCAG 1.4.13 and is not an accessible way to show required information.

Suggestion

Option 4, with the table headings as they are. The repetition is the problem. The table shows each hint once, in the column heading, and the cards can do the same with one panel. It removes 24 lines of text without adding 24 buttons, which options 2 and 3 do. It also needs no script.

If research shows people need the hint right beside the label, option 2 is the one to build, with a fallback that shows the hint as plain text when JavaScript is off. Option 3 is simpler to build, but its in-place expansion uneven the cards.

Still to decide

  • Which option. Decided: option 4. If research shows people need the hint beside the label, option 2 is the one to build.
  • Open by default. Closed for now. If people do not open it, try it open, or put the four lines in the intro text.
  • Test it. Ask what "Control measures" means before showing any hint, then see whether people look for the explanation, and where.
