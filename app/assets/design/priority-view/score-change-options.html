Option C · For the design team, not for participants

Showing when a priority score changed

Today a changed score gets a tag and the old score ("Score decreased, previous score 16"). It does not say when it changed. A score that has not changed says nothing at all, so a score that has not moved for 6 months looks the same as one that was checked yesterday.

What we need to show

  • When a changed score changed, and what it was before.
  • When an unchanged score last changed, so people can tell "stable" from "not looked at".
  • What moved, if we can. Now the score is risk × impact, only those two inputs can move it. Control measures and response options sit beside the score and do not change it.

Why a single date is not simple

The two inputs that move the score update at different rates. These rates are assumptions for this page, so please correct them.

Input How often it changes (assumed)
Risk of incursionMonthly, and straight away if a case is confirmed in the UK
ImpactAbout every 6 months, or sooner if new evidence arrives
Priority scoreRecalculated whenever risk or impact changes

So "last changed" depends on which input moved, and a slow input can leave a score looking old when it is simply stable. Every mock-up below uses the same six diseases and dates, and today is 11 November 2026. Each shows a change tag only for changes in the last 30 days, which is also an assumption. Option 1 and Option 2 apply that rule. Option 3 replaces it with a window the person picks.

Option 1: a date under every score

Every row gets a date line in the Priority cell. Changed scores read "Changed 5 November 2026" and keep the tag and previous score. Unchanged scores read "Last changed 12 May 2026". The pattern is the same in the table and on the cards, and no new column is needed.

Good for

  • Answering both questions in one place, for changed and unchanged scores
  • The smallest change to Option C, with no new column and no new control
  • Telling "stable for months" from "changed this week" at a glance

Watch for

  • The priority cell gets taller, and every row now has a third line
  • It does not say what moved, only when. The reason would be on the disease page
  • The date can't be sorted, so "show me what changed recently" is not possible

Option 2: a "Last changed" column, with what moved

A separate column gives the date of the last change and says which input moved. It works for every row, changed or not. People can sort by it to bring recent changes to the top. The Priority cell keeps its tag and previous score for recent changes, and has no date.

The mock-up is not sorted, so you can read down the rows in priority order. When someone sorts by the new column, the order becomes 5 November, 3 November, 29 October, 6 October, 7 September, 12 May.

Good for

  • Answering "what changed recently?" by sorting
  • Saying what moved, so people don't have to work it out from the risk and impact columns
  • Keeping the Priority cell short

Watch for

  • A seventh column in a table that was already cramped. It would mean narrower columns or sideways scrolling
  • The cards have no column, so they would still need a date line, as in Option 1
  • The reason text needs writing, or generating from the data in a consistent form
  • The date sits apart from the score it describes

Option 3: compare over a period you choose

People choose what to compare with, such as last month, last quarter or last 6 months. Each score shows its net change over that period, and the start date is stated above the table. Scores that have not changed say so for that period, and no date is shown on them.

Good for

  • No arbitrary "30 days" rule. People pick the window that matches what they care about
  • "Since when?" is always stated, so there is no ambiguity about what the tag compares with
  • The same table works for a monthly check and a quarterly review

Watch for

  • It adds a control, and people have to choose before they see anything useful
  • It shows only the net change. A score that went up and then back down looks unchanged
  • An unchanged score gets no date. Rainbow tail disease just says "no change in this period", and does not say it last changed in May
  • The window would need to be remembered between visits, and shown clearly on the cards too

Suggested next step

Try Option 1 in Option C first. It is the smallest change, it works the same in the table and on the cards, and it covers both parts of the question. Keep Option 2's "what moved" wording and the sortable column as a follow-up, if participants ask what changed rather than when.

Questions for the team

  • What does "last changed" mean? The last time the score value changed, or the last time risk and impact were checked, even if the score stayed the same? The second is probably more useful for trust, but is harder to explain.
  • How long should the tag stay? This page assumes 30 days. A slow input, such as impact, could leave a changed score looking old, and a monthly input would show the tag all month.
  • Do we have the dates? Every option needs the date of each input's last change, and the previous score, for every disease.
  • Do control measures and response options need dates too? They don't move the score, so they are kept out of this page. They could carry their own "last reviewed" date, and that is a separate question.

Probes for the test

  • "Which of these scores changed most recently?" Does the date answer this without prompting?
  • "Do you trust a score that last changed in May?" Does the date make it look stale, or stable?
  • "What would you want to know about a change?" Do they ask for the reason, the date, or both?
