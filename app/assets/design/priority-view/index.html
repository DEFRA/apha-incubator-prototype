Summary

Sprouting hoof disease is unlikely to reach the UK, but it would have the greatest impact and we are least prepared for it. That makes it the top priority, followed by Himalayan Dragon Pox and Rainbow tail disease. Wing wobble is likely to reach the UK, but we are well prepared, so we only need to maintain that readiness.

What has changed. In the latest assessment (October 2026), no disease changed rank or priority band. The score for Himalayan Dragon Pox fell from 4.4 to 4.1, and the score for Wing wobble rose from 1.2 to 1.4. The other three are unchanged. Changes in risk, impact, control measures and response options are on their own pages.

What the priority bands mean

  • Act now Score of 4.5 or more. The main focus for resources.
  • Prioritise Score of 3.5 to 4.4. Close the preparedness gaps.
  • Monitor Score of 2.5 to 3.4. Review readiness and watch the risk.
  • Maintain Score under 2.5. Keep current arrangements.
How the priority score is worked out

Priority is a score out of 5. A higher score means we should act sooner. It brings together four scores, each out of 5.

  • Risk of incursion: how likely the disease is to reach the UK. 5 is the most likely
  • Impact: how much harm it would do if it arrived. 5 is the most harm
  • Control measures: how well we can stop it entering. 5 is excellent
  • Response options: how well we can deal with it if it arrives. 5 is excellent

A well-prepared disease scores lower even when the threat is high. A poorly prepared disease stays near the top even when it is unlikely to arrive.
