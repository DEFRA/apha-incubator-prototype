Scoring framework this design must respect

From the journeys and mapping board. Four layers of evidence combine into one priority score per disease.

LayerGroupScaleSource
1 Risk of incursionThreatLow 1, Medium 2, High 3IDM monthly report, Risk ID report
2 ImpactThreatHigh 3, Very high 4, plus what is affected (animal health, economy)D2R2 data, disease modelling, impact scoring
3 Control measuresVulnerability0 to 10 (10 = excellent): how well we can prevent entryPreparedness indices
4 Response optionsVulnerability0 to 10 (10 = excellent): how well we can respondPreparedness indices

Priority = (Risk + Impact) × (20 − Control − Response), out of 140, ranked 1 to 5.

Act now 70+Prioritise 45 to 69Monitor 20 to 44Maintain under 20

Illustrative mapping to the fictional names: Sprouting hoof disease (priority 75), Himalayan Dragon Pox (55), Rainbow tail disease (50), Fuzzy fox flu (42), Wing wobble (18).

What the levels mean

1A Cards, sorted by risk Test

Baseline. Source stated once under the heading.

Tests

  • Sorting by risk beats A to Z.
  • Text tags beat arrows.
  • Can users find the changes unaided?
  • Do users read risk and impact as two different things?

Variants to try

  • Source link on every card instead of once.
  • "Up 1 level" wording.

Design team questions

  • Impact has "what is affected" (animal health, economy). Is that worth a line on the card?
  • Does impact change over time, or is it fixed until reassessed?

1D Ranked by priority Design team only

Shows how the overview grows once all four layers exist. Priority becomes the default sort.

Shows

  • Rank, band and score lead. The four layers sit underneath as one line.
  • One plain sentence per disease explains the "so what" (UN10).
  • Colour belongs to the priority band, so change tags stay neutral.

Design team questions

  • Who writes the sentence and how often?
  • Do users trust a combined score or need to see the working first?
  • Do we show the 0 to 10 scores as numbers, bars or both?
  • How do the five IDM risk levels map to the framework's Low, Medium, High?
  • Keep 1A to 1C as the layers view behind a "View" switch, or replace them?

2C Priority and four layers Design team only

Shows how the detail page grows. Each layer links to its own history or detail.

Shows

  • Priority and the plain-language "so what" first.
  • Four layers grouped as the framework groups them: Threat and Vulnerability.
  • The working shown on demand, so the score can be trusted (UN1).
  • Each layer keeps its own source, date and history, using the screen 2A or 2B pattern.

Design team questions

  • Is the layer a section on one page or a separate page?
  • Preparedness is scored so higher is better, but vulnerability is 20 minus it. Show the gap, or the score?
  • Does priority have its own history over time?
