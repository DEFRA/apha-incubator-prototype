Next steps for design

What the design team does next, in and after round 2.

Before the next session

  • Lock the view for each task. Let a link say whether to show the table or the cards, and hide the toggle, so participants stay in the view they are given.
  • Get the ten preparedness actions from the team, so the “What are these actions?” link can lead somewhere (see questions for the team).
  • Agree the wording of the preparedness labels, the placeholder group names and the risk page card titles.
  • Check the hosted site’s audience before pushing, since real disease names are used.
  • Test with a screen reader (VoiceOver or NVDA): linked headings, the “i” buttons, navigation and the hidden table summary.

Card sorting exercise

To find the best labels for the “slices”: risk, impact and preparedness, and the two parts of preparedness.

Card sort plan
QuestionNotes
What goes on the cards?The pieces of information people have asked for, such as time frame, source and evidence, how it spreads, current policy, and the preparedness actions. Wait for the round 2 answers to the “What would you expect to see here?” pages before writing them.
Open or closed?An open sort asks people to group the cards and name the groups, which finds labels we haven’t thought of. A closed sort gives them our labels and checks them. Suggested: open first, then closed on the labels that come out of it.
Which labels?The slice names; the two preparedness groups, which are placeholders; and possibly the scale labels, including the “not at all prepared” question.
Who takes part?The same kinds of people as the research sessions, including people who work with the preparedness measures. To be agreed with Tess.

Possible for round 3

Depends on what round 2 shows.

  • Definitions for risk, impact and preparedness levels, if people still need them.
  • Alerts and change history, depending on Tess’s follow-up with participants.
  • Sorting and filtering, if a clear need emerges.
  • Impact and preparedness pages for each disease, once we know what people expect on them.
  • A product name, once users have said how they would describe it.
  • How big the preparedness gaps are and how long they take to close.
  • Several threats at once, early signs and a governance forum. These are for research first, not design.
