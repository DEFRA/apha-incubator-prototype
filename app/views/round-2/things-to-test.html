Things to test

What the round 2 prototype can show participants, with the link for each. The questions behind these are on open questions for research.

Tasks the prototype can support

Round 1 had three tasks. Keeping them, with the new diseases, is the simplest way to stay comparable. Tess is drafting the scenarios. The prototype has an answer for each of these:

  • Say what the page is telling you and which disease is the top priority.
  • Say what to prioritise preparing for, using the table.
  • Say how prepared we are for a disease, from the status and then the breakdown.
  • Say why the African swine fever risk is very high.
  • Say what changed on African swine fever.
  • Say what else they would expect under Impact and Preparedness.

Success measures to define with Tess: task completion; naming the highest priority disease; reading preparedness the right way round; saying why the risk is high; saying what changed.

Things to watch for

  • One view per task. Ask participants to stay in the view they are given, and alternate which view comes first. Cards show by default on small screens, so test on a large screen. Locking the view is still to be built.
  • Change versus alerts. We are testing whether people can read a change, not whether they want to be told about one.
  • Grouping on the risk page. Ask about the three cards on the risk page (rating, where it comes from, how it could change): what is missing inside each, and is a whole card missing?
  • Hidden table summary. A short summary is read by screen readers before the table. It is an assumption until tested, so compare with and without it in the screen reader sessions.
  • Other diseases. Only African swine fever has detail pages. The other four link to a “not part of this test” page.
  • Source links are an optional lens. They are off by default, so the main test assumes no case management access or reference. The links go to a stand-in page. Ask what people would expect to find there, and whether they would use it, rather than judging the link design.
  • Illustrative data. A bar at the top of every page says the disease names are real and the ratings are invented.
