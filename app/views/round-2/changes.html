What changed in v2, and why

Every change since the round 1 debrief, with the reason. All of it is in v2, so round 1 v1.1 stays as the starting point. The next iteration, v2.1, comes after the team has given feedback. Items marked “to confirm” are built but not yet agreed by the team.

Reading the priority view

Priority view changes
ChangeWhyStatus
An “i” beside Priority, Risk of incursion, Impact and Preparedness, showing the levels in order. Priority explains the score and its labels.Labels weren’t clearly understood and the key sat at the foot of the page.To confirm
Sorting removed. Column headings link to pages for risk, impact and preparedness. Navigation renamed “By priority”, “By risk”, “By impact” and “By preparedness”.People read headings as links, and there was no clear need to sort.To confirm
Preparedness stays one status, with “What makes up this status” for a breakdown into two groups.People wanted more than one score but didn’t understand the old split.To confirm
The breakdown reads “x of 10 actions complete” and “y actions still to do”, with a link to what the actions are. Light red when fewer than half are complete.The bare “1 of 10” didn’t say what was counted, and a mostly red bar was read as progress.Agreed (wording open)
A percentage version, opened by a link in the black bar at the top of the priority view.To compare with the count, knowing neither shows effort or importance.Agreed
The real top five diseases, with invented ratings, and an “Illustrative data” bar on every page.More believable for participants without using restricted data.Agreed
A visually hidden summary before the table, linked with aria-describedby.Tables are hard for screen reader users. To test with and without it.Proposed
The two “What the … mean” sections removed from the priority view. The same information is in the “i” buttons.It repeated the “i” content.To confirm
Table and cards both kept. The risk “More details” link for African swine fever goes to the risk page.The team agreed to test both before deciding.Agreed

Detail pages

Detail page changes
ChangeWhyStatus
The cross-disease risk, impact and preparedness pages are plain “What would you expect to see here?” prompts. The old topic lists are in the researcher notes.To hear expectations without leading people.Agreed
African swine fever disease page: about the disease, a risk summary linking to the risk page, and plain prompts for impact and preparedness.Starts with one disease, where the team thinks users begin, and shows whether a summary is enough.To confirm
African swine fever risk page, in cards: rating, where the rating comes from, how it could change, and changes to the rating.Uses only confirmed topics, so it tests content rather than a wish list. Grouping shows what is missing.Agreed
Other diseases link to a “not part of this test” page.So nobody wonders whether the prototype is broken.To confirm

Showing a change

Risk is the measure that changes, so only African swine fever’s risk rating shows a change.

  • A blue “Changed” tag beside the rating, at the far right of the row on the disease and risk pages, so a change is quick to spot. On the priority view it sits under the rating, in both the table and the cards.
  • “Was High until 3 November 2026” under the rating on the disease page.
  • A “Changes to this rating” table on the risk page, with date, rating and reason. It is what makes the risk page worth opening.
  • No arrows. An up arrow is bad news for risk but good news for preparedness, which would confuse.

The options we compared are in the design workings (not published here).

Not changed

  • Alerts: waiting on Tess’s follow-up with participants.
  • Sorting and filtering: deferred until there is a clear need.
  • The key at the foot of the page, kept so we can see whether the inline “i” gets used.
  • The product name “Disease threat intelligence”. “Dashboard” is avoided. We will ask users what they would call it.
