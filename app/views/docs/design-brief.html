Design brief: risk and impact prototype

This is an earlier document. It was written for a test of risk and impact on their own, which the team has replaced with a test of the priority view (see the notes for the user researcher). Where it describes impact, it uses earlier labels. The agreed impact areas are Public health, Animal welfare, Wider economy and society, and International trade. Any scoring formula in it has also been replaced. Priority is now risk × impact, out of 25, with the priority actions Act now (15 or more), Prioritise (10 to 14), Monitor (4 to 9) and Maintain (1 to 3). Control measures and response options are checklists and do not change the score. The final design is option C of the priority view.

Project: APHA incubator – animal disease early warning Status: Draft for team review · Test round: 5 working days Design system: GOV.UK Design System only (alpha phase banner, standard header and footer)

Problem

Subject matter experts (SMEs) get disease information from separate sources, such as the International Disease Monitoring (IDM) monthly report, then rework it by hand. They can't quickly see which threats matter, what has changed and why. We want to test whether one trusted view answers this.

The early warning framework has four layers: risk of incursion, impact, control measures and response options. The priority score is risk multiplied by impact. Control measures and response options sit beside it as checklists and do not change it. Risk is ready and impact is being gathered. Preparedness (control measures and response options) comes later, so the design must be able to grow into it.

Users

SMEs and policy/operational leads who prioritise threats and plan responses.

Needs we will test (🔥 on the mural)

Need numbers and titles follow the consolidated nine user needs (9 November 2026). The twelve earlier needs were combined into nine. See "The nine user needs" in the design log.

Need Question the prototype must answer
UN1 One trusted source Is this a trusted, formal source, and the single, up-to-date one?
UN2 Early warning signal Has the risk changed, in which direction, and how is it changing over time?
UN3 Where it's spreading and UN6 Early enough to prepare Which threats are likely to reach the UK, and when?
UN4 Highest-priority diseases Which threats matter least? (Fully answered only by the priority score, a design-team option for now.)
UN8 Right level of detail Can I get a short operational summary?

Goal and success signals

Users can say, within a minute and unprompted: which disease is highest risk, which changed most recently, which has the greatest impact, and where the information came from. - Users find the latest changes without help. - Users tell risk and impact apart, and see that a low risk can still have a very high impact. - Users say what they'd do next ("so what").

Scope

Test round: risk and impact. Two screens: (1) disease overview, (2) disease detail with risk history. Illustrative data for five fictional diseases. Design team only: how the design grows to the priority score and all four layers (wireframe options 1D and 2C). Out: preparedness data in the test, real disease names, map view, geographic risk.

Hypotheses to test

  1. Coloured text tags ("Risk increased") work better than arrows. The priority view now uses words always, with a small arrow in the table and a tag on cards.
  2. A "Latest changes" view helps more than sorting alone.
  3. Sorting by risk beats A–Z.
  4. Stating the source once, with a link, builds enough trust.
  5. The change history with reasons answers "why?".
  6. Users read risk and impact as two separate things.
  7. Cards and list views suit different tasks.

Constraints and risks

  • GOV.UK components only. The risk meter and line chart aren't standard components, so each needs a text alternative, a data table alternative and accessibility testing.
  • Data is illustrative. Risk means likelihood of incursion and ignores geography. State both so users don't read them as real.
  • Scales are not agreed. The workshop used five risk levels. The priority view now rates risk and impact from 1 to 5 and shows them as matrix names (Rare to Almost certain, Insignificant to Severe). Control measures and response options are checklists, with assumed totals of 10 and 8 items. All of this needs SME agreement.
  • Colour: an increase in priority is bad. Priority actions (Act now, Prioritise, Monitor, Maintain) carry colour and are written in sentence case. Change arrows and tags are for priority only. The checklists use red for items to review and no green.
  • Hiding "no change" diseases could hide a high risk. Test it, don't assume.

Disease names (illustrative)

Sprouting hoof disease · Himalayan Dragon Pox · Rainbow tail disease · Fuzzy fox flu · Wing wobble. The priority view adds a sixth, Scaly claw fever, which is confirmed in the UK.

Open questions

  • Can all users open the IDM report link?
  • How do five IDM risk levels map to the framework's three?
  • Does impact change over time, or is it fixed until reassessed?
  • Who writes the plain-language "so what" for each disease, and how often?
  • Is there more than one risk source (e.g. WOAH)?
  • How does preparedness (control measures and response options) link into priority, now that it does not change the score?
  • What does "Changed [date]" on a priority score mean: the score changed, or the inputs were last checked?
