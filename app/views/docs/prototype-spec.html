Prototype spec: risk and impact, screen by screen

This is an earlier document. It was written for a test of risk and impact on their own, which the team has replaced with a test of the priority view (see the notes for the user researcher). Where it describes impact, it uses earlier labels. The agreed impact areas are Public health, Animal welfare, Wider economy and society, and International trade. Its scoring formula and score out of 140 have also been replaced. Priority is now risk × impact, out of 25, and control measures and response options are checklists that do not change the score. The final design is option C of the priority view (see the test plan). Sections 2, 3 and 6 are updated to match.

Companion to design brief and risk and impact wireframes. Sources: Concept collaboration workshop and the journeys and mapping board (5 Nov 2026). All data is illustrative. Diseases are fictional so real names don't distract users.

The test round covers risk and impact only. Options marked design team only (1D, 2C) show how the design grows to the priority score and all four layers. They are not part of the test.

1. Global elements (every screen)

  • GOV.UK header with service name "Disease threat intelligence".
  • Alpha phase banner with a feedback link.
  • Standard GOV.UK footer.
  • Page title pattern: [Page] – Disease threat intelligence – GOV.UK.
  • Sentence-case headings and disease names.
  • Breadcrumbs, not a back link: Home > Diseases.
  • GOV.UK Frontend components wherever one exists.

2. Scoring framework (from the journeys and mapping board)

Layer Group Scale Source
1 Risk of incursion Threat 1 to 5, shown as a name: Rare, Unlikely, Moderate, Likely, Almost certain. A disease already in the UK shows "Confirmed in the UK" IDM monthly report, Risk ID report
2 Impact Threat 1 to 5, shown as a name: Insignificant, Minor, Significant, Major, Severe. Plus what is affected (Public health, Animal welfare, Wider economy and society, International trade) D2R2 data, disease modelling, impact scoring
3 Control measures Preparedness (does not change the score) A checklist of 10 items (assumed). Shows "N of 10 need review" and a bar with red segments for the items to review. How well we can prevent entry Preparedness indices
4 Response options Preparedness (does not change the score) A checklist of 8 items (assumed). Shows "N of 8 need review" and a bar with red segments. How well we can respond Preparedness indices

Priority score = risk × impact, out of 25. Risk and impact are each rated 1 to 5. Control measures and response options do not change the score. Nothing is ranked or numbered. Diseases are sorted by score, highest first. Priority actions: Act now 15 or more · Prioritise 10 to 14 · Monitor 4 to 9 · Maintain 1 to 3. The cut-offs, the checklist totals and the update cadences are assumed.

Because preparedness sits beside the score, a poorly prepared disease with a low threat stays low. How preparedness should link into priority is an open question.

Impact has no change history in the data we have, so the test shows it as a level and a statement only.

4. Screen 1: Disease overview

URL: /diseases Purpose: Show which diseases are highest risk, what changed, how big the impact would be, and where the data came from. Needs: UN1, UN2, UN4, UN8.

Layout (top to bottom)

  1. Breadcrumbs.
  2. H1 "Diseases".
  3. Source sentence: "Risk is from the IDM monthly assessment report, October 2026. Impact is from APHA impact scoring." The report name is a link.
  4. Controls: view toggle (Cards | List) and a Sort by select (Highest risk · Lowest risk · Most recent change).
  5. Disease cards or table.
  6. Details: "What the levels mean" (risk and impact definitions).

Disease card (option 1A)

Element Content / component
Card title Disease name, linked to screen 2 (visually hidden text keeps link text unique)
Risk Risk meter (5 bars) plus the word, e.g. "High"
Change GOV.UK Tag: "Risk increased", "Risk decreased", "No change", with "from Medium" in plain text. Text only, no arrows
Impact Level plus what is affected, e.g. "High (economy)"
Last assessed Date

Rules: - Cards stay equal height and width. - Colour is never the only signal: tag wording stays. - Reading order: title → risk → change → impact → date. - One column on mobile. Two columns on desktop.

Latest changes (option 1B)

Two tabs: "All diseases (5)" and "Latest changes (2)". The sentence under the heading says how many risk levels changed. The latest changes tab lists increased and decreased diseases. Tab 2 ends with "3 diseases had no change in risk. View all diseases."

List view (option 1C)

GOV.UK table. Columns: Disease · Risk · Change · Impact · Last assessed. Disease name is a link. Includes a checkbox "Hide diseases with no change in risk", default off.

Interactions and states

  • Sort keeps the chosen view. Announce changes in an aria-live="polite" region ("Sorted by highest risk").
  • No JavaScript: the sort select uses a submit button.
  • Empty state: "No diseases match."

Variants to test

Open questions

  • Does hiding "no change" diseases risk hiding a high one? Default the filter to off.
  • Is "what is affected" worth a line on the card?
  • Does a change in impact belong in "Latest changes"?
  • Check the layout with more than five diseases.

5. Screen 2: Disease detail

URL: /diseases/wing-wobble Purpose: Explain why the risk is at this level, what changed, how big the impact is, and what to do. Needs: UN2, UN3, UN6, UN8.

Layout, option 2A (chart and change history)

  1. Breadcrumbs: Home > Diseases > Wing wobble.
  2. H1 "Wing wobble".
  3. Summary list: Risk (meter, word, change tag, "from Medium") · Impact (level and what is affected) · Last assessed · Source (link to the October 2026 report).
  4. What this means: the "so what", 1–2 sentences.
  5. Risk over time: line chart, one dot per monthly report. Buttons: 6 months · 12 months · All. "Show as table" in a Details component.
  6. Change history: GOV.UK table with Month · Risk and change · Reason for change. Checkbox "Show months with no change" (default off).
  7. Impact: level and a short statement.

Layout, option 2B (summary first, table only)

  1. Breadcrumbs and H1.
  2. Two boxes side by side: Risk (level, change, date, source) and Impact (level, what is affected, date).
  3. What this means.
  4. Why the risk changed.
  5. Risk history, last 12 months: table of every month, with changes highlighted. No chart.

Chart requirements (2A)

  • Y axis: the levels in order, labelled with words, not just colours.
  • Single line. Don't compare diseases in this round.
  • Text summary above the chart: "Risk rose from Medium to High in October 2026."
  • Keyboard-operable points. A table alternative is always available.
  • Colours pass 3:1 contrast for graphics.

Interactions and states

  • Time buttons update the chart and the table caption.
  • The checkbox filters the history rows. No JavaScript: all rows are shown.
  • Print: chart and table both shown.

  • Is a statement enough for impact, given it has no history?
  • Is the risk source always IDM? If WOAH is also used: add a Sources column and a filter.
  • How to show multiple strains and an outbreak already in the UK.
  • Real data is needed for "What this means" and the reasons.

6. Design team only: growing to priority and all four layers

Wireframes: 1D Ranked by priority and 2C Priority and four layers. Not part of the test. The priority overview is now built as option C of the priority view. The bullets below describe option C. The wireframe names are kept so they can be found. Options A and B of the priority view are not updated and are out of line with C.

1D Overview

  • H1 "Diseases by priority". One page with a "View as" switch between a table and cards. Sorted by priority score, highest first. Nothing is ranked or numbered.
  • Table columns: Disease, Priority, Risk of incursion, Impact, Control measures and Response options. Each heading has a short hint (for example "Risk × impact, out of 25").
  • Disease name is plain text, with a "More details" link under it. For the two Act now diseases the link reads "Act now: see why this is a priority".
  • Priority cell: a priority action tag in sentence case, "Score 20", for a change "Up from 12" or "Down from 16" with a small arrow at the end of the line, and "Changed [date]" on every row.
  • Risk and impact show as names (for example Almost certain, Major). A disease already in the UK shows a purple "Confirmed in the UK" tag with the date and the number of premises.
  • Control measures and response options show "N of M need review" and a bar with red segments. There is no colour block and no score.
  • Cards sit two across at 64em and wider, grouped by priority action. Each shows "Priority score 20 out of 25", "Previous score" and "Changed [date]" with an outlined "Score increased" or "Score decreased" tag for a change, a one-line reason (e.g. "Unlikely to arrive, but the impact would be severe."), a "More details" link and the four measures, each with its own "More details" link.
  • At the bottom: a key ("What the priority actions mean" and "What the four measures mean"), then how the priority score is worked out and where the information comes from. There is no summary at the top and no "Look in more detail" section.

2C Detail

  • Priority headline: priority action, score out of 25, any change and the date, then the plain sentence.
  • "How the score is made": risk and impact, which make the score. Beside it, control measures and response options, which do not change it. Each links to its own history or detail, which reuses the 2A or 2B pattern.
  • Details: "How the priority score is worked out" with the formula.

Decisions to make before building beyond risk

  • Scales: option C uses five levels for risk and impact, shown as matrix names. Agree them with the framework owners.
  • Colour: priority actions carry colour. Change arrows and tags are for priority only, where an increase is worse, so up is red and down is green. Changes in the checklists need a rule when they are designed.
  • Preparedness display: chosen. Checklists with "N of M need review" and red segments, with no score. How preparedness links into priority, now that it does not change the score, is still open.
  • Per layer: its own scale definition, source, last-assessed date, reason for change and "what this means". Don't assume monthly IDM reporting.
  • Priority history: does the score have its own history over time? What does "Changed" mean: the score changed, or the inputs were last checked?
  • Ownership: who writes the plain sentence, and how often.
  • Naming: keep "Diseases" as the page name so it doesn't need renaming later.
  • Not confirmed: impact and preparedness scales and data, the checklist totals, the priority action cut-offs and the update cadences. All values here are invented.

7. Test plan summary (5 working days)

Task Screen Measure
"Which disease is highest risk right now?" 1 Time, correct answer
"What has changed since last month?" 1 Finds changes, tab usage
"Which disease would be worst if it arrived?" 1 Finds impact, separates it from risk
"Which disease would you worry about least, and why?" 1 Does a low risk with a very high impact surprise them?
"Where does this information come from? Do you trust it?" 1 / 2 Finds source, trust comments
"Why did the risk for Wing wobble go up?" 2 Finds reason
"How has it changed over the year?" 2 Uses chart or table
"What would you do next?" 2 Understands the "so what"

8. Accessibility checklist

  • Colour is never the only signal (tag text, risk word).
  • Risk meter has a text alternative ("High, 5 of 5" etc.; final scale to be confirmed).
  • Link text is unique or has a visually hidden suffix.
  • Chart has a table alternative.
  • Works at 320px wide and at 200% zoom.
  • Works with keyboard only and with a screen reader (test VoiceOver).
