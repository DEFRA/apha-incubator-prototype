After the team design review

12 November 2026. The team reviewed the priority view. These changes make priority view prototype v1.1, in two versions. Every change below is in both versions. The only difference is how preparedness is shown. The test leads with combined preparedness, and the separate version is to show if combined is flagged as not working for people. More updates from the review will be added here.

Changes after the team design review
Change Why
Scaly claw fever is removed, and with it the "Confirmed in the UK" state The service will not cover diseases that are already active in the UK, so a disease that has arrived does not belong in the priority view. Five fictional diseases remain, and only one is in Act now
The test leads with combined preparedness. Separate preparedness is the version to show if combined is flagged as not working The team decided combined preparedness is the better starting point. The separate version stays ready as the alternative. The combined page is the main entry point (index.html) and the separate page is separate.html
Preparedness is tested two ways. Control measures and response options stay as two separate columns, and are also combined into one preparedness measure with five statuses: Totally prepared, Largely prepared, Moderately prepared, Slightly prepared and Not at all prepared We do not yet know whether people need prevention and response shown separately, or only an overall status. The combined version replaces the count of items with a status. Each is its own screen, and they link to each other
The priority score combines all four measures The priority scoring team member said priority should reflect all four columns, not only risk × impact. The prototype uses a fictional process that mirrors the earlier one and adds both preparedness measures: threat (risk × impact) multiplied by 1 plus a preparedness gap, out of 60. The real scoring will be worked out later. The design only needs to show that all four are considered
Risk of incursion has six levels: Very high, High, Medium, Low, Very low and Negligible These replace the earlier names (Rare to Almost certain). A sixth colour, orange for Medium, was added so each level has its own
The impact areas are removed (Animal welfare, International trade, Wider economy and society) It was not clear what they added at this level
The change display is removed: the "Changed" date, the previous score, the arrows and the increased and decreased tags It rested on too many assumptions about what change matters. Research will ask what change people want to see, why, and what they would do with it, and we will design from there. The earlier options are kept on showing when a priority score changed

Knock-on changes: the priority actions now use cut-offs out of 60 (30, 20 and 8), the key and the "how the score is worked out" panel were rewritten, and the reasons on the cards were reworded to match the new level names. The decisions are in the design decisions.

Signalling priority without prescribing action

To review 12 November 2026. Raised in the team design review. Not applied to prototype v1.1.

The priority actions (Act now, Prioritise, Monitor and Maintain) are not very helpful at an individual level. Some people need to act on a disease and others don’t, depending on their role, so telling everyone to "Act now" wrongly attributes action. But the column still has to get attention, because the CEO does need to organise action. We came up with eight ways to signal priority that do not prescribe an action that is not relevant to everyone.

Options for signalling priority without prescribing action
Option The idea
1. Say where it sits Keep the coloured tags, but say Highest, High, Medium or Low priority instead of an action
2. Numbered priority groups A neutral label, Priority group 1 to 4, with a key that describes scores and not action
3. A score bar No category. The score and a single-colour bar, so the longest bars draw the eye
4. Emphasise only the top group Only the highest scores get a heavy edge and a label. Everything else shows just the score
5. Show what is driving the score "Driven by: very high risk, not at all prepared", so each role sees whether their part is involved
6. A marker for leadership attention A separate "For leadership attention" marker on the top group. It names who needs to organise action and doesn’t tell everyone else to act
7. A view for your role People pick a role and the column signals what matters to that role. Needs the real roles, from research
8. Just the score, styled by band No categorisation language. Each disease shows its score, coloured by group. We ask users what they would call the groups and what they would group by, and the language comes from them

Our view is that the options work better in pairs. 4 with 5 gets a leader’s attention and lets each role find their part, and 6 now with 7 later fits if research shows several roles need different signals. The comparison has the others. Option 8, just the score styled by band, was added because the team wants to see no categorisation language at all, and to ask users what they would call any grouping. This is for the team and the user researcher to decide.

What we have learned from designing

These are design learnings from making and comparing options. They are not research findings. Research learnings from each round will be added under the round.

Design learnings
Learning Where it led
One summary at the top can’t be short and right for everyone We removed the top summary and kept a one-line reason on each card
A rank number gave false precision We removed the rank and sort by priority score
Hints under every label made the cards busy A key at the bottom of the page explains the measures once. See card hint options
Red against green fails for colour blindness, and a high risk and a high preparedness score pull in opposite directions Control measures and response options show a count of items to review with dark red segments. See checklist options
Showing a change needs a date. Otherwise a stable score looks the same as one nobody has checked Every score says when it changed. See showing when a score changed
A tag, a score and a change are too much to read as three styles Plain lines in one style under the tag. See priority cell options
"Bands" is a word we made up, and capitals are harder to read "Priority actions", in sentence case
Link wording promises something. "Disease details" reads as facts about the disease, and "what to do now" promises content we may not have "Act now: see why this is a priority" for the two Act now diseases, and "More details" for the rest
A label that tells people what to do is wrong for people who don’t need to do it The priority actions are under review. Eight ways to signal priority without prescribing action are in signalling priority
Showing change needs more than a date, and we were guessing what change matters After the team design review we removed it, and will ask users what change they want surfaced and why
A long date line squeezed the most important column, the disease name The Disease column has a minimum width and the date sits at the bottom of the cell in smaller text

The nine user needs

The user needs we agreed on 26 October 2026 were twelve, in three themes. On 9 November we combined three pairs that overlapped, giving nine needs in the same three themes. The evidence behind each need is held by the user research team and is not published here.

  • UN1 One trusted source combines the earlier "One trusted source" and "One shared source". Both described one place everyone trusts that tells them when something changes.
  • UN2 Early warning signal combines "When risk changes" and "Agreed trigger point". The signal is in scope. Agreeing what happens when it changes, and who acts, is organisational, so that part sits with governance.
  • UN8 Right level of detail combines "Short operational summary" and "Right level of detail". Both were about getting information at the depth each role needs.

The other six carry across unchanged and are renumbered.

Wider open questions

These cut across rounds. Questions about one part are on that part’s page: priority view and risk exploration.

  • Brand. The prototype uses a Defra look: system font, green links, and a black header with a green stripe. The brief says GOV.UK only. Which do we put in front of users?
  • "Preparedness" or "vulnerability"? The scoring framework uses "vulnerability". The prototype says "Preparedness". Do we test both?
  • Real data. Which diseases have which impact areas, and what were the previous scores and change dates? All of it is made up in the prototype.
  • Internal source names. The prototype names internal data sources (IDM, D2R2, Risk ID and the preparedness indices) in "Where this information comes from". This is a public repository. Is that fine, or should we generalise them before the next push?
